One real source trail

The source is the first stop, not the answer.

Walk the same research Markov used to move from a demographic fact to a testable market idea and a finished script opening.

Starting source

How could Japan's ageing population reach the U.S. Treasury market?

This begins as a question, not a premise Markov must defend. The research can reject the framing, narrow it, or follow a better intermediary.

NextSeparate the demographic fact from the market story.
Established

Japan is ageing rapidly.

Japan’s Statistical Handbook reports that people aged 65 and over were 29.3% of the population in 2024 and projects 38.7% by 2070.

Inspect the Statistics Bureau source, page 28

What the source leaves out

A demographic shift does not tell us who saves, who invests, or which constraint controls the portfolio.

The missing intermediary is institutional: households, insurers, pensions, banks, and official institutions translate saving into different assets under different rules.

Research branchFind evidence for the saving channel and identify the actual asset holders.
Evidence-backed interpretation

Ageing can change saving and cross-border asset allocation.

A 2024 Bank of Japan analysis, with explicit model caveats, finds that longer lives can raise aggregate saving and push the current account toward surplus. U.S. Treasury data separately show Japan held $1.024 trillion in long-term U.S. Treasuries at June 2025.

Inspect the BOJ analysisInspect the Treasury survey
Plausible hypothesis

Watch the hedge before the headline holding.

BOJ evidence shows Japanese insurers reduced currency-hedged foreign bonds when hedging costs rose. The first observable adjustment may therefore appear in hedge ratios and FX-swap demand—not in aggregate Treasury holdings.

Could break ifHedging stays stable while Treasury flows change for unrelated reasons.
Inspect the BOJ Financial System Report, page 28

Script opening · followed from this connection

Japan is getting older. That sounds like a story about pensions and hospitals. But one of its earliest market signals may appear somewhere less obvious: in the price Japanese institutions pay to hedge U.S. bonds.

The script keeps the demographic measurement, the model caveat, the Treasury exposure, and the unproven causal step attached for fact-checking.
