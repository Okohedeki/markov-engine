What Markov finds

The opportunity is usually between the things everyone covered separately.

Markov maps the information landscape before it recommends a direction. It shows why an angle appears underexplored instead of calling it “original.”

  1. 01Repetitive answers

    Questions where articles, search results, and AI answers keep returning the same explanation.

  2. 02Missing connections

    Important subjects covered separately but never combined into a useful mechanism.

  3. 03Ignored audiences

    Questions your audience asks that broad, general coverage does not resolve.

  4. 04New consequences

    Recent developments that make an old assumption newly important—or newly wrong.

One complete transformation

A demographic story becomes a capital-markets idea.

This example uses a real Japan source packet. It separates observed information from Markov’s interpretation and keeps the missing evidence visible.

  1. 01
    Signal

    Japan’s pension pivot puts overseas capital in play.

    A policy proposal suggests more domestic allocation. It does not demonstrate a Treasury sale.

    REUTERS / JUL 10, 2026Policy catalystRead source ↗
  2. 02
    Existing landscape

    Most coverage stops at “aging creates pressure.”

    It repeats demographics, debt, and a possible selloff without naming the institutions or portfolio mechanics between them.

    DemographicsHigh

    Fiscal pressureHigh

    Institutional flowsLow

  3. 03
    Unexpected connection

    Domestic yields and currency hedging can change the buyer decision before any sale.

    NBER research supplies the missing mechanism: mandates, duration, relative yields, and USD/JPY hedging sit between a demographic trend and an asset flow.

    Higher JGB appeal→Weaker hedged returns→Less new foreign demand
  4. 04
    Idea opportunity

    Japan does not need to dump Treasuries to change the U.S. financing story.

    The underdeveloped angle is marginal demand: who replaces Japanese buying if gradual reallocation makes overseas bonds less attractive?

    Information gainHighConnection rarely developed

    Audience relevanceStrongTimely for policy audiences

Limits stay visible. A formal allocation change and aligned flow data could strengthen—or weaken—this interpretation.

Why Markov does not write in your place

Your point of view is the product.

  • Landscape and saturation analysis
  • Unexpected, typed connections
  • Structured argument and story beats
  • Counterarguments and open questions
  • Channel-native treatments and sequence
You contribute
  • The final thesis you stand behind

  • The decision to publish
