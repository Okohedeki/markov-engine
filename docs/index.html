Script idea / The human storyEditorial example

The hardest part of recycled water might not be the filter.

On a space station, recycled water is a necessity. Back on Earth, a brewer put it in a beer. Could the next challenge be getting people to take a sip?

What makes this a different story

Move from a technical milestone to a story about trust: what makes an unfamiliar idea feel ordinary?

Open the script outline ↓
  1. Open in orbit: NASA reported a 98% total water-recovery demonstration in 2023. Establish the constraint: supplies cannot simply be topped up on a long mission.
  2. Cut to a brewery: Epic Cleantec describes a beer project using treated shower and laundry water from a San Francisco high-rise, with Devil’s Canyon Brewing Company.
  3. Follow the human question: the company says the project challenges perceptions of recycled water. Interview drinkers and an independent water specialist before claiming it changes minds.

Keep this boundary: This is a thematic comparison, not a claim that the brewery uses NASA hardware. A company account does not establish public acceptance.

Script idea / The hidden infrastructure storyEditorial example

Why a chip factory has a space-station problem.

A space station and a chip factory both need water to come back. But their recycling numbers do not tell the same story.

What makes this a different story

Connect spaceflight to the infrastructure behind chips, then investigate what the impressive recycling percentages actually measure.

Open the script outline ↓
  1. Start with the shared constraint: keeping water in use matters when bringing in more is difficult. Use the NASA article to introduce the space example.
  2. Follow it to Arizona: TSMC describes a water-reclamation plant expected in 2028, with a target of at least 90% recycling. Present this as a company plan, not a completed result.
  3. Make the comparison useful: ask what each percentage includes, how much fresh water is still required, and what treatment costs. Get comparable figures before drawing a conclusion.

Keep this boundary: Do not equate the two systems, their water standards or their percentage denominators. No shared technology or NASA-to-TSMC transfer is established by these sources.
