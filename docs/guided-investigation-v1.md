# Guided investigations — V1

## Product and positioning

Markov helps creators and independent writers find overlooked, source-backed
story leads without handing their point of view to a content generator.
Best fit: someone with a source worth exploring and a voice of their own.
Poor fit: automatic publication, guaranteed exclusives, or an exhaustive search
of every account on every platform. Full talking points and series remain paid.

The promise is a guided investigation: follow a lead, check the connection,
then make it yours. The mechanism must be visible, not sold as random deep search.
The creator gains material to explore, confidence about its limits, and room to
become a distinctive voice in their subject. Novelty is relative to the seed,
not a guarantee that nobody has ever made the connection.

## Working UI direction contract

- Preserve the current public design and guided-source workspace; this is copy
  and evidence disclosure, not a redesign. Keep existing DM Sans, spacing,
  cool neutrals, restrained orange actions, and source imagery.
- Memory hook: a question follows a located passage into a qualified story lead.
  Show this through readable questions and citations, not a decorative graph.
- Hierarchy: useful angle first; connection, competing explanation and next check
  inside its existing disclosure; optional research trail below the ideas.

  references in guided-source-direction.md; no new visual direction is needed.
- Check desktop, tablet, narrow mobile, keyboard disclosure and reduced motion.

## Engine contract

- Initial questions name a tentative hypothesis, missing evidence and a possible
  disconfirmation. Follow-up questions must cite inspected parent evidence.
- Reserve half the research deadline and six of twelve reading attempts for
  follow-up. Search decisions carry a purpose; previews never become evidence.
- Read source content across existing media adapters; video requires a download.
  A failed read or unavailable platform remains a visible limitation.
- Separately assess proposed connections against the passages. Reject causal
  leaps, keyword coincidences and unsupported assertions. Distinguish a report,
  interpretation and hypothesis; assess source independence conservatively.
- Store a small case-scoped graph of questions, passages, sources and leads in
  the existing discovery event. Typed links record provenance and model
  assessments, not inferred real-world causation. No graph database or new service.
- Preserve existing cases and owner boundaries. Cached historical cases are not
  silently upgraded or rerun. Model review is not independent fact verification.

## Research inspiration, not a benchmark claim

- [Literature-based discovery](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232891):
  explore possible connections, then investigate the missing links.
- [IRCoT](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.557/) and
  [MindSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20183): findings guide later questions.
- [PropRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18070) and
  [HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802): retain context with relationships.
- [STORM](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347/): multiple perspectives,
  with explicit caution about source bias and over-association.

V1 does not implement these systems wholesale, graph neural networks, global
PageRank, or exhaustive path search. Its graph records the bounded investigation;
the planner follows explicit evidence references. Compare against adaptive
no-graph search on supported novelty, creator usefulness, latency and cost before
adding graph-based retrieval. These papers do not prove journalistic quality.

  invalid evidence IDs, conflicting support, conservative independence,
  reading reservations, typed graph links, review caveats and legacy exports.
