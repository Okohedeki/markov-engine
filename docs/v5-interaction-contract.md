# Markov V5 interaction contract

## Product progress

- **Trigger:** A content lead has a source, question, trend, or half-formed observation but cannot tell whether it contains a publishable contribution.
- **Functional progress:** See what current answers repeat, identify a missing connection, choose an audience-relevant contribution, and carry it into a development plan.
- **Emotional progress:** Replace blank-page uncertainty with a specific decision that can be inspected and challenged.
- **Social progress:** Publish work that contributes a recognizable point of view instead of another competent summary.
- **Dream:** Markov becomes the working layer where a team develops its editorial advantage over time.

## Direction brief

1. **Problem:** Professional publishers need to decide what is worth adding because search results, AI answers, and competitor coverage make the existing landscape easier to repeat than to extend.
2. **Primary outcome:** A visitor should operate one realistic comparison and leave understanding how Markov turns a crowded answer into an underdeveloped idea.
3. **Visual thesis:** An editorial decision instrument reveals one layer at a time—existing answer, missing connection, audience need, contribution—using square fields and semantic color rather than floating consumer cards.
4. **Content plan:** interactive opportunity hero → controllable answer-to-opportunity scene → audience fit → complete source workflow → campaign treatments → AEO/authorship boundary → CTA.
5. **Interaction thesis:** The visitor changes the comparison lens in the hero, advances or scrolls through the reasoning sequence, and chooses among competing directions; motion preserves continuity between those decisions.
6. **Constraints:** FastAPI/Jinja, progressively enhanced HTML, lightweight vanilla JavaScript, no scroll hijacking, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, reduced-motion equivalence, real Japan example, hosted-SaaS positioning, and no finished-writing promise.

## Art-direction contract

- **Design thesis:** Markov feels like a publishing instrument in use, not a dashboard screenshot or an animated mood board.
- **Memory hook:** Four familiar answer lenses converge; the visitor opens the indigo seam between them and selects the contribution that should be developed.
- **Composition:** The first viewport is one asymmetrical working surface. The next act pairs a quiet decision rail with a sticky transformation stage. Later sections remain shorter so the proof scene owns the page.
- **Typography:** Serif carries the proposed editorial contribution; sans and mono carry controls, sources, state, and reasoning. Hero scale remains dominant, while live product text stays readable at 12px minimum.
- **Color logic:** Vermilion is input and action, ink is existing landscape, indigo is connection and opportunity, teal is audience relevance. Every state is named in text as well as color.
- **Material language:** Comparison lanes, evidence strips, mechanism arrows, editorial annotations, state counters, and development briefs. Motion uses transforms and opacity only to reveal causality and continuity.
- **Forbidden defaults:** no Recall-style pill navigation, blue gradient environment, floating entertainment cards, looping card drift, feature carousel clone, fake typing, decorative cursor, generic product screenshot, or animation without a product-state change.

## Critical vertical slice

### Opportunity Explorer

- Four tab controls: Search results, AI answers, Competitor coverage, Previous work.
- Each lens reveals the dominant answer, what it omits, and the opportunity produced by that omission.
- Arrow keys, Home, and End change the active lens.
- The selected lens remains visible without relying on color.

### Answer-to-opportunity scene

- Three controllable stages: Existing answer, Missing connection, Choose contribution.
- Scroll observation may advance the stage on desktop, but buttons remain the primary explicit controls.
- Three distinct directions update a shared decision panel containing thesis, audience promise, why now, and what could weaken it.
- Mobile becomes a normal sequential flow with no sticky trap.
- Reduced motion removes automatic scroll-driven state changes and keeps direct controls functional.

## Acceptance checks

- A first-time visitor can state what Markov compares, what changes, and what they receive after operating the first viewport.
- The hero and story controls work with pointer and keyboard input.
- State updates are exposed through selected controls, visible labels, and a polite live region.
- The page remains fully understandable without JavaScript.
- The interaction demonstrates Markov's specific job and could not plausibly sell a generic knowledge base.
- Desktop, tablet, and mobile have no overflow, clipped controls, sticky collisions, or inaccessible motion.
