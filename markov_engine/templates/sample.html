Inspectable sample · published sources

From Japan’s ageing population to a question about Treasury hedging.

This is not proof of one long causal chain. It is a demonstration of how Markov follows an idea while keeping each step at the evidence level it has earned.

Established

Starting node: Japan’s population is getting older.

The Statistics Bureau’s 2025 handbook reports that people aged 65 and over were 29.3% of Japan’s population in 2024. Its projection reaches 38.7% in 2070.

What this establishes: the demographic shift. It does not, by itself, establish a market outcome.

Shared mechanism · longevity and saving Evidence-backed interpretation

Ageing can alter aggregate saving and the current account.

A Bank of Japan Research Lab article uses overlapping-generations models and explicitly warns that results depend on assumptions. Its simulations indicate that longer expected lives can increase aggregate saving and that ageing may have pushed Japan’s current account toward surplus.

Mechanism
Longer expected retirement changes saving behavior and capital allocation.
Weakens
The result is model-dependent; ageing does not imply a simple, permanent fall in saving.
Could lead to
Inspect which sectors hold the surplus and which domestic or foreign assets receive it.
Dependency link · Japanese portfolios and U.S. debt Established exposure

Japan is materially exposed to U.S. securities, including Treasuries.

The U.S. Treasury’s annual survey reports $2.883 trillion in U.S. securities held from Japan at June 2025, including $1.024 trillion in long-term Treasury debt. The survey is a position measurement, not evidence that demographics caused those holdings.

Mechanism
Japanese households do not buy every bond directly; banks, insurers, pensions, and funds transmit portfolio choices.
Weakens
Custody and survey classification limit what country totals reveal about the ultimate investor.
Could lead to
Separate insurers, pensions, banks, and official institutions before making a demand claim.
Second-order consequence · currency hedging Evidence-backed interpretation

The relevant constraint may be the hedged return, not the headline Treasury yield.

The Bank of Japan’s October 2023 Financial System Report says life insurers reduced currency-hedged foreign bonds as foreign-currency funding and hedging costs rose, and that hedge ratios declined substantially.

Mechanism
A yen-based institution evaluates foreign bonds after the cost of reducing currency risk.
Weakens
Insurers are only one investor class, and the cited period reflects a particular rate environment.
Could lead to
Compare hedged yields, insurer hedge ratios, and net Treasury transactions on aligned dates.
Plausible hypothesis

Resulting insight: watch the hedge before the headline holding.

If demographic change alters the institutions managing Japanese savings, the first observable market adjustment may be in hedge ratios and FX-swap demand—not necessarily a clean fall or rise in aggregate Treasury holdings.

Why this is useful: it turns a vague macro story into a testable research design.

What would falsify it: stable hedging behavior across investor groups while Treasury flows change for unrelated reasons.

Next research packet: sector-level foreign-bond holdings, hedge ratios, USD/JPY hedge costs, and TIC transactions on the same frequency.
