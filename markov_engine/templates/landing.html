    MARKOV BRIEF5 COLLECTED SOURCESSOURCE ROLES ATTACHED

    Why would Japanese investors sell U.S. Treasuries?

    Japan did not act as one seller. The live catalyst is a proposal to steer public pension capital toward domestic assets; the mechanism is that higher Japanese yields and costly currency hedging can make overseas bonds less attractive to specific institutions.

    Bottom line

    A gradual reduction in foreign-bond demand is plausible. A coordinated Treasury dump is not established, and the policy proposal does not yet specify the size, timing, or assets involved.

    What matters
    • In July 2026, Japan’s finance minister proposed increasing domestic allocations at the $1.8 trillion GPIF and other retirement funds.
    • Reuters reported that higher JGB yields had become comparable to—or better than—Treasuries after currency hedging.
    • The NBER paper shows why policy rates, duration, and USD/JPY hedging costs sit between a demographic story and an asset sale.
    What the question leaves out

    “Japan” includes a central bank, pension funds, insurers, banks, households, and private funds. They hold different foreign assets and do not rebalance as one actor.

    Claim status

    EstablishedA domestic-allocation proposal was floated.

    EstablishedJapanese and hedged U.S. yields have repriced.

    InterpretationSome portfolios may repatriate capital.

    UnresolvedWhether Treasuries are sold, by whom, and how much.

    MARKOV RESEARCH REPORT2 PATHS COMPAREDEVIDENCE ATTACHED

    The policy signal came before the sale.

    The dramatic interpretation turns a proposal into a completed Treasury liquidation. The stronger path predicts slower, institution-specific repatriation that may first appear in allocation targets, hedging choices, and marginal foreign-bond demand.

    Stronger path

    Domestic-allocation proposal → higher JGB appeal → weaker hedged foreign returns → gradual portfolio rebalancing.

    The Reuters report supplies the live catalyst. The NBER paper supplies the rates, duration, and currency-hedging mechanism. Neither alone proves a Treasury sale.

    Competing path

    Aging → fiscal pressure → forced national liquidation.

    The FT film supports the demographic context, but this path skips the institutions that own assets and the mandates governing what they can sell.

    What would change the conclusion

    A formal GPIF allocation change followed by aligned sector-level outflows and Treasury transaction data would move the claim from plausible mechanism to observed event.

    MARKOV SCRIPTREADY TO RECORDFACT NOTES ATTACHED

    Japan did not decide to dump America’s debt.

    A documentary opening built from the same five-source chain, with the strongest mechanism and its limits still attached.

    OPENJapan did not wake up and decide to dump America’s debt. In July 2026, its finance minister floated something narrower—and potentially more important: steering the world’s largest pension fund toward more Japanese assets.

    TURNThat does not prove a Treasury sale. But it changes the question. If Japanese government bonds now compete with U.S. bonds after currency hedging, why should every overseas allocation stay where it is?

    MECHANISMA pension fund or insurer compares liabilities, domestic yields, and the cost of turning dollars back into yen. Higher JGB yields plus an expensive hedge can make foreign bonds less attractive, leading to gradual rebalancing or simply fewer new purchases.

    CLOSEThe evidence supports a mechanism and a catalyst—not a dump. The next signal is not a dramatic headline. It is a formal allocation change followed by the flow data that shows which assets actually moved.
