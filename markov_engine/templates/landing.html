One source, still moving

A useful source should open a path—not close the question.

This real Japan example begins with a policy signal. Markov separates what happened from what people infer, then finds the missing link worth following.

1

Starting source

Japan’s pension pivot puts overseas capital in play.

A Reuters report establishes a proposed domestic-allocation shift. It does not establish a Treasury selloff.

Article · ReutersPolicy signal

Proposal and timing are visible. The aggregate flow effect is still an interpretation.

Saved to the Japan Chain
2

Separate the claim

“Japanese investors will dump Treasuries” is several claims wearing one coat.

Markov breaks the dramatic conclusion into institutional decisions that can be inspected independently.

Domestic yields become more competitive. Portfolio mandates can change allocation. New demand can weaken before holdings are sold.
3

Expose the skipped step

Who becomes the buyer before anyone becomes the seller?

The useful story is not the loudest prediction. It is the financing question hidden between demographic pressure and a market outcome.

Open question

Which institution changes the marginal flow—and what data would reveal it first?

4

Follow the connection

The first Treasury signal may be the buyer who never arrives.

Higher domestic yields, mandate changes, and hedging costs can weaken marginal buying before a visible sale. The connection stays labeled as an interpretation until aligned flow data supports it.

Working question

Who absorbs gradually weaker Japanese demand?

Track the institutions most likely to replace marginal buying, then compare auction demand and sector-level holdings before treating a proposal as a sale.

Why it matters
A slower buyer can change financing conditions before the headline event appears.
Where it may break
Domestic allocations may change too slowly or be offset by another buyer.

A deliberate fit

For work that has to hold up after it leaves the draft.

Best for

People who publish factual work.

  • Research-led creators and editorial teams
  • Analysts, strategists, and consultants
  • Teams turning recurring research into briefs, reports, or scripts
  • Agent builders who need inspectable research state
Not for

Work where the source trail is optional.

  • Generic high-volume copy or social filler
  • Fiction and unconstrained creative writing
  • Passive read-later or personal-archive workflows
  • Replacing the human decision to publish
