A real source-to-insight path

The first answer is not the interesting part.

This example uses published data from Japan’s Statistics Bureau, the Bank of Japan, and the U.S. Treasury. The final step remains a hypothesis—and is labeled that way.

Established

Japan is ageing rapidly.

Japan’s Statistical Handbook reports that people aged 65 and over were 29.3% of the population in 2024 and projects 38.7% by 2070.

Inspect the Statistics Bureau source, page 28
Evidence-backed interpretation

Ageing can change saving and cross-border asset allocation.

A 2024 Bank of Japan research note, with explicit model caveats, finds that longer lives can raise aggregate saving and push the current account toward surplus. U.S. Treasury survey data separately show Japan held $1.024 trillion in long-term U.S. Treasuries at June 2025.

Inspect the BOJ analysisInspect the Treasury survey
Plausible hypothesis

The market signal may show up in hedging behavior before headline Treasury holdings.

BOJ evidence shows Japanese insurers reduced currency-hedged foreign bonds when hedging costs rose. That makes hedge ratios and FX-swap costs a useful early research branch—but the demographic-to-hedging chain is not established by these sources alone.
