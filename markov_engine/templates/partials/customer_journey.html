Before your first link

A little context
before you create.

Is this a summarizer?

No. The original link is a starting point for finding connected stories and fresh script directions. Source context helps you judge the connection; it is not the finished product.

What makes this a guided investigation?

Each search has a question to answer and evidence to look for. Inspected passages can prompt a follow-up question. Markov then assesses proposed connections against those passages, considers competing explanations, and keeps the research trail attached. It is not an unlimited search or a guarantee of a new angle from every link.

Does a connection mean the story is proven?

No. A shared name, a sequence of events or several posts repeating one source is not proof. Leads show their limitations and a next check. The assessment uses a model, not an independent human fact-checker; inspect the original sources and decide what you can responsibly say.

Can I see it without an account?

Yes. The interactive source-to-story example and captioned walkthrough are open to everyone. They show a curated example, including a preview of paid talking-point depth—not a free live-generation tool.

Will any link work?

Start with a public article, video, podcast or post. Retrieval depends on the source and your workspace’s supported extractors. Private pages, unavailable transcripts and access restrictions can prevent research. Markov does not bypass them.

What does Story Mode make?

A reusable series plan from 2–30 selected story ideas: give it a premise, order its episodes and track recording progress. Generate each episode’s talking points separately. Markov does not film or automatically publish your videos.
